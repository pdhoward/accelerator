# Demo script: the AI Cradle (Monday 2026-11-09)

About 13 minutes plus questions. Every query below was tested on 2026-11-08. It is told as one story: *a store that couldn't understand its customers, rebuilt so the business's own rules run it, and proven before it goes live.*

## 30 minutes before

1. **Set the stage for the gate story. Don't retire SCN-3 before the demo; you retire it on stage.** Open app.strategicmachines.ai → Machine Shop → Policy → Conformance gate. The card should read **"held: 1 failing"**, and the only failing case should be **SCN-3** ("Product page offers Buy now with Stripe checkout").
   - **Rehearse it once today, not on Monday:** press **Prove again** *without* retiring anything. Within seconds the card should show "being proven now", and within about a minute "held" again, with only SCN-3 failing. That proves the Vercel side works.
   - If any other case fails, tell Claude before Monday.
   - **Don't retire SCN-3 in rehearsal.** Once the rules pass they are in force for good, and the story is used up. If that happens, ask Claude to reset the gate (put SCN-3 back and clear the version in force); it takes a minute.
2. **Warm up C.** Open https://machineshop-git-cradle-strategicmachines.vercel.app and run one search. The first request after a deploy takes about 7 seconds while the store compiles its rules; after that, searches take about 2 seconds.
3. **Voice (optional).** Make one short call on C with the access code. If it misbehaves, leave voice out.
4. **Open these tabs in order:**
   1. app.strategicmachines.ai/platform
   2. A, the traditional store: https://machineshop-git-baseline-strategicmachines.vercel.app
   3. C, the AI-run store: https://machineshop-git-cradle-strategicmachines.vercel.app
   4. Control Room → Machine Shop → Policy

## The talk track

**1. The problem (1 minute).** Every company's business rules are buried in code: pricing, eligibility, what the site promises. Nobody has the full list, and changing one rule takes a developer, a ticket and a release. Legacy systems, COBOL included, are the extreme case.

**2. The idea (2 minutes), on /platform.**
- Headline: *"Transform your business from running on software to running on AI. Intelligence matters."*
- Scroll to **"How a Cradle app comes to life, and how it runs"** and walk left to right:
  - your legacy app, in any language, is read, not rewritten;
  - the Accelerator turns its code into a policy book the business owns, and proves every change;
  - the new app runs in your own cloud, with your services where they are.
- **The shift:** the app boundary moves. The language model becomes the app's router and rule handler, the "operating system". Verified code (kernels) still does anything that must be exact: money, stock, checkout. The screens are built from tested components.

**3. A against C (4 minutes): the "wow".** Put A and C side by side and type the same thing into both:
- *"a cordless drill under $150 I can buy today"*: A finds **nothing**. C finds one, explains why, and shows its filters.
- *"compare brass pendant lights"*: C builds a **comparison table**.
- *"something to keep my garage floor clean"*: C understands the need and broadens its search.
- Press **Why?** on C. It shows the steps the agent took (route, ground, decide, execute, render), the time, the cost (a fraction of a cent) and **the business rules that shaped the answer**.
- In C's assistant, type *"I want to buy this drill"*. It takes you to the product page and tells you to press Buy now, exactly as the owner's ruling says. Optional: the same by voice.

**4. The rules, and a story about trust (4 minutes), in the Control Room.**

*Set it up.* "The question I always get: how can you trust an AI to run your business? Here's our answer."

- **Policy.** The store's rules, in plain English, each one versioned and dated. When the crew read the old code, it found contradictions: the code said one thing in one place and something else in another. The owner settled each one with a ruling. Read one aloud: *"a product can be bought only when its stock status clearly says it's in stock."*
- **The Conformance gate.** "Each rule change ships with its evidence. Before new rules reach a single customer, the app is tested against scenarios written from the rules, and critical ones must pass three runs out of three. Until they pass, the last proven rules stay in force."
- **The card says "held: 1 failing".** "So right now the newest rules are *not* live. Let's see why." Open the scenarios and click **SCN-3**. Read it: *"The product page offers Buy now with secure Stripe checkout, and doesn't say the store can't take orders."*
- **The twist.** "The AI crew wrote this test from the rules, and it's wrong. Our store runs Stripe in test mode, so the product page truthfully says 'no real charge'. The test reads that as 'this store can't take orders'. The app isn't misbehaving; the test is."
- **The human decides.** "This is the part I care about most. The machines draft the tests, but a person decides what 'correct' means." Press **Retire** on SCN-3.
- **Prove again.** Press **Prove again**. The card switches to **"being proven now"** within seconds. Nobody touched the store: the Accelerator told the app to prove itself.
- **Fill the minute while it runs.** "Right now the app is running every scenario: can an out-of-stock product be bought? Does the assistant point a buyer to Buy now? Does the order page tell the truth in test mode? Critical ones run three times. A model marks the plain-English ones, and the exact ones are checked by code."
- **The payoff.** The card turns **in force**, with every scenario passing. "Now these rules are live, with the evidence attached. That's what lets a business trust an AI-run app: you don't trust the model, you check the proof."
- One more true line, if you want it: "This gate caught real problems this week. The assistant wasn't reliably sending buyers to Buy now. It was fixed, then proven."

**5. The honest numbers (1 minute), /platform → "Measured".**
- Lighthouse scores are the same for both stores.
- C answers plain-language requests that A can't.
- C costs about 1.5 seconds more and a fraction of a cent per request.
- A rule change takes about a minute, against days to weeks.

**6. Where this goes (1 minute).**
- **The app that polices itself:** like a car calling the dealer for service, it watches the regulations its rules cite and tells the owner when a change is needed, with the proposed new wording and its tests.
- **Every channel from one app:** the same answer on the web, in Slack or Teams, or by voice.
- **Less code to own:** in Machine Shop about 70% of the code is channel plumbing that the Cradle shares across apps.

## If something goes wrong

| What happens | What to do |
|---|---|
| C's first search is slow | Talk through it. "It's compiling the owner's rules", which is the point. |
| C shows the ordinary product grid instead of an answer | The agent call failed and the store fell back to classic search. Run the query again. |
| Voice doesn't connect | Skip it. The typed assistant shows the same behavior. |
| After **Prove again**, the card doesn't move to "being proven now" within about 20 seconds | Open C in another tab and load any page; that also starts the proof. Keep talking through the "fill the minute" lines. |
| The proof takes longer than about 90 seconds | "The app is proving its rules in the background, and the store keeps serving the last proven version meanwhile. That's the point." Move on to the numbers, and come back to the card at the end. |
| The proof ends **held** on a case other than SCN-3 | Even better: open it and read why. "The gate just stopped a rule change that wasn't proven. That's exactly its job." |
| Before the demo, SCN-3 is already retired and the card says **in force** | Ask Claude to reset the gate before you present. On stage, if needed, tell it in the past tense: "Yesterday the gate held these rules on a bad test; I retired it, and here is the proof." |

## Likely questions

- **"Isn't the model unpredictable?"** That's why the gate exists. The behavior is measured, not trusted: every rule change ships with its evidence, and money, stock and checkout run in verified code.
- **"What does it cost?"**
  - **Running the store:** about $0.0003 per AI search, roughly $3 per 10,000.
  - **Changing a rule:** about a quarter, once.
    - Compiling the new rules costs about $0.02, once per rules version on each running server. Shoppers never trigger it.
    - Proving them costs about $0.20 per full run of today's 10 scenarios, once per rule change (or per Prove again). It grows with the suite, at about $0.02 per scenario per run.
- **"Where does it run? Are we locked in?"** The app runs in your cloud. Next, the proven rules are baked into each deployment, so the app keeps running even if the Accelerator is down.
- **"Our systems are COBOL."** The same pipeline already reads mainframe estates. AWS's public CardDemo has been ingested in slices, read-only.
- **"Who changes the rules?"** The business owner, in plain English, in the Policy Walk. No developer.
- **"What about our data and keys?"** Secrets live in an encrypted vault, and the AI never sees them. The crew starts read-only.
